How Shawnee prices work

Four rules that keep the bill easy to understand.

These are business policies, not sales slogans. The point is to make it easier for you to decide what you want done without guessing what will show up on the invoice later.

01

Estimate before repair

We explain the proposed repair and expected cost before ordinary repair work begins. When a job changes, the estimate is not treated as permission to keep adding work.

02

Parts at actual cost

Parts are billed at what Shawnee actually pays to obtain them. We do not add a percentage or house markup simply because the part passed through us.

03

Approve added work first

If teardown, testing or the repair uncovers something else that changes the plan, we stop and talk to you before proceeding with additional work or cost.

04

Itemized finish

When the authorized work is complete, the invoice should show what was done and what you are paying for rather than hiding everything inside one unexplained number.

Estimate vs. diagnosis

The $25 estimate is simple. Complex diagnosis stays honest.

The standard estimate fee is $25. If Shawnee Auto Repair performs the repair, that $25 fee is waived in full.

Some problems are straightforward enough to inspect, identify and estimate during the normal estimate process. Others — especially intermittent electrical, drivability, wiring or hard-to-reproduce problems — can require additional testing time before anyone can responsibly say what is wrong.

If deeper diagnostic work is needed:

We tell you what additional testing is recommended and what it will cost before that diagnostic work proceeds. The $25 estimate is not used as permission to run up diagnostic time without your approval.

Parts pricing

No parts markup means no parts markup.

Traditional repair pricing often includes a markup on parts. Shawnee’s model is different: the part cost is kept transparent and the labor charge pays for the labor.

Parts at actual cost.

When Shawnee purchases a part for your repair, you are billed what it actually costs us to obtain that part rather than an added percentage markup.

  • Budget, standard and premium choices can be discussed when useful options exist.
  • Major parts are identified on the estimate or invoice so you can see what was used.
  • Unavoidable supplier, shipping or similar charges actually incurred to obtain a part are not hidden as “markup.”
  • If a refundable core charge is later credited back to Shawnee, that credit belongs in the customer’s final accounting too.
What “actual cost” means:

The amount actually required to obtain the part for your repair, including unavoidable supplier charges when they exist — with no extra percentage or house markup added by Shawnee simply for reselling the part.

Special-order or nonreturnable parts

If a job requires a part that cannot reasonably be returned or canceled, we tell you before it is ordered. Any exception to the ordinary no-upfront-payment policy must be discussed and approved first.

Customer-supplied parts

Ask first. Customer-supplied parts may be considered case by case depending on fitment, quality, safety and the job. Warranty responsibility can differ when the part was purchased elsewhere.

Part warranties

Manufacturer and supplier warranty terms vary. We’ll pass along the warranty information that applies to the part used. Any Shawnee labor or workmanship coverage offered for a specific repair will be stated with that job rather than implied as a blanket promise.

Authorization

The repair should never outrun the conversation.

Our policy is simple: when the job changes, we contact you before additional work becomes part of the job.

Illinois law gives consumers specific estimate and authorization rights. Shawnee’s day-to-day approach is to keep the conversation even simpler: if there is a meaningful change in the repair plan or added work is needed, you should hear about it before we continue.

1

We identify the proposed repair

You receive the repair plan and expected charges required for the work being proposed.

2

You authorize the work

Approval can be documented in the manner appropriate for the job before the authorized repair moves forward.

3

Something changes? We stop and contact you.

Unexpected damage, additional failed components, extra diagnostic needs or other changes are discussed before they become additional authorized work.

4

You receive an itemized invoice

Completed work, parts and applicable authorized charges are shown when the job is finished.

Shawnee Repair Assistance

Need the repair, but money is tight?

Tell us before you assume the repair is out of reach. When someone is genuinely in a bind, we are willing to look at the job and see whether there is a responsible way to make it more manageable.

That does not mean every repair can be discounted, financed or done for whatever amount is available. It means we are willing to have the conversation instead of automatically treating the first estimate as the only possible path.

There is no automatic qualification or guaranteed discount.

Assistance is entirely case-by-case and depends on the vehicle, the repair, parts cost, safety, our ability to absorb some of the cost and the circumstances involved. Any different payment arrangement or reduced price must be clearly agreed to before it applies.

Payment & exceptions

Nothing due upfront for ordinary repair work.

That promise should be useful, not vague. Here is what it means — and what happens when a job has unusual parts, outside services or cannot be completed exactly as planned.

Ordinary Repairs

We generally do not require a deposit for ordinary repair work. Payment is due when the authorized work is completed and the itemized invoice is provided, before the vehicle is released unless another arrangement has been agreed.

Special Orders

If a customer-approved job requires a specifically ordered, nonreturnable item or outside service that requires advance payment, any exception is explained before the order or commitment is made.

Towing / Transport / Outside Services

Third-party towing, transport, machine work or other outside services are not silently folded into a repair. If they are needed, the expected charge or arrangement is discussed before authorization whenever practicable.

If the Repair Cannot Be Finished

You are not charged repair labor that was not performed. Authorized diagnostic work, completed authorized work, installed parts, specifically ordered nonreturnable parts or approved third-party charges may still apply.

No hidden storage policy:

Shawnee does not treat leaving a vehicle with us as permission to create an undisclosed storage charge. If a particular situation could require storage fees, the terms would need to be disclosed before they apply.

Illinois consumer rights

Our policies do not replace your rights under Illinois law.

Illinois has specific rules for automotive repair estimates, authorization, invoices and unauthorized work. Shawnee’s policies are intended to work alongside those protections, not reduce them. For the official consumer guidance, use the Illinois Attorney General’s automotive repair information.

Pricing questions

The fine print, without hiding it.

What if I really need the repair but cannot afford the whole bill?

Tell us. There is no guaranteed hardship discount or payment program, but we are willing to look at the situation with you. Depending on the repair, that might mean prioritizing the most important work, choosing a more affordable responsible part, postponing non-urgent work, reducing some labor when we are able, or considering another clearly agreed arrangement case-by-case. If you are in a genuine bind, do not be embarrassed to ask.

Is the $25 estimate always the only diagnostic charge?

No. The standard estimate is $25 and is waived if Shawnee performs the repair. Some complex or intermittent problems require additional diagnostic time or testing. If that is needed, the additional diagnostic work and cost are discussed and authorized before proceeding.

What happens if I get the estimate and decide not to repair the vehicle?

If no additional diagnostic work or other authorized charges were involved, the standard $25 estimate fee is due. If you authorized deeper diagnostic work, nonreturnable special-order parts or a third-party service, those authorized charges may also apply.

Does “parts at actual cost” mean the cheapest part available?

No. It describes how the part is priced, not which quality level you must choose. When useful options are available, we can discuss budget, standard and premium choices and the tradeoffs between them.

Do you mark up shipping or supplier fees?

No percentage or house markup is added. If an unavoidable shipping, supplier or similar charge is actually required to obtain the part for your repair, it can be passed through as part of the actual cost rather than hidden inside an inflated part price.

What if you discover another problem after work starts?

We contact you before additional work becomes part of the job. We explain what was found, what we recommend and how it changes the expected cost so you can decide whether to proceed.

Do I have to pay upfront?

For ordinary repair work, generally no. Payment is due when the authorized work is completed. A special-order/nonreturnable part or outside service may require a different arrangement, but any exception is discussed before you authorize it.

What if a part fails later?

Warranty coverage depends on the part, supplier and circumstances. If a part carries a manufacturer or supplier warranty, those applicable terms can be reviewed. If you ever have a concern after a repair, contact us so we can inspect the issue and determine what happened.

Can I bring my own parts?

Possibly. Customer-supplied parts are considered case by case. Fitment, part quality, safety and warranty responsibility all matter, so ask before buying or bringing the part.

Questions before you authorize?

Ask us before the wrench turns.

If anything about the estimate, part choice, labor, discount, repair-assistance options or repair plan is unclear, ask. We would rather explain it before the work than argue about it after the invoice.
