What we work on

Common services, explained clearly.

Exact availability depends on the vehicle, the specific repair and whether the job is a good fit for on-site work or our repair location. If you are unsure, send the year, make, model and symptoms and we’ll start there.

Diagnostics & Electrical

When the symptom is the clue, not the answer.

Electrical and drivability problems can waste money fast when parts are replaced before the fault is understood. We start with the symptoms, testing and what the vehicle is actually doing.

  • Check-engine and warning-light diagnosis
  • No-start and intermittent-start concerns
  • Stalling and running problems
  • Battery-drain / parasitic-draw concerns
  • Wiring, power and ground faults
  • Electrical accessory and circuit problems
  • Sensor and related circuit diagnosis
  • Scan-tool codes used as a starting point — not a parts list

Starting & Charging

Battery, starter or alternator? Test it first.

Slow cranking, clicking, dead batteries and charging problems can overlap. Testing the system before replacing components helps separate the actual failure from the symptom it caused.

  • Battery testing and replacement
  • Starter diagnosis and replacement
  • Alternator / charging-system diagnosis
  • Battery cables and terminals
  • Ground and connection problems
  • Intermittent no-crank concerns

Brakes

Noise, vibration, pulling or poor braking.

Brake work should be based on what is worn, damaged or not operating correctly — not automatically replacing everything at once when it is not needed.

  • Brake pads and rotors
  • Calipers and related hardware
  • Brake hoses and visible leak concerns
  • Grinding, squealing and vibration diagnosis
  • Pulling or uneven braking concerns
  • Brake inspection before repair

Cooling, Belts & Hoses

Overheating and leaks need a cause, not a guess.

Cooling-system problems can turn into expensive engine damage if the vehicle keeps being driven hot. We look for the source of the leak, circulation problem or failed component before recommending the repair.

  • Overheating diagnosis
  • Coolant-leak concerns
  • Thermostats
  • Water pumps
  • Belts and tension-related concerns
  • Cooling and other service hoses

Steering & Suspension

Clunks, looseness, wandering and worn components.

Suspension and steering wear often shows up as noise, looseness, poor handling or uneven movement. We inspect the components involved and explain what is actually worn before replacing parts.

  • Tie rods
  • Ball joints
  • Control arms
  • Struts and shocks
  • Sway-bar links and related wear items
  • Wheel-bearing / hub concerns when applicable

Audio, Camera & Accessory Installation

Clean installation matters as much as making it turn on.

Aftermarket electronics should work without creating new wiring problems. We can handle common upgrades and troubleshoot installation-related issues when the vehicle and equipment are a good fit.

  • Head units and touchscreen radios
  • CarPlay / Android Auto upgrades
  • Backup-camera installation
  • Speakers and common audio upgrades
  • Bluetooth and microphone installation
  • Audio and accessory wiring concerns

Pre-Purchase Inspections

Look before the vehicle becomes your problem.

A used vehicle can look great and still hide expensive problems. A pre-purchase inspection gives you another set of eyes before money changes hands.

  • Visible mechanical-condition review
  • Warning lights and scan-tool check when applicable
  • Leaks and obvious wear concerns
  • Steering, suspension and brake observations
  • Test-drive observations when safe and appropriate
  • Plain-language explanation of what stands out

A pre-purchase inspection can reduce surprises but cannot guarantee that every hidden, intermittent or future defect will be discovered.

General Repair & Maintenance

If it is not listed, ask.

Not every repair fits neatly into one category. We also take on general mechanical work and routine maintenance when the job fits our equipment, schedule and the vehicle involved.

  • General mechanical repairs
  • Routine maintenance by request
  • Gauge-cluster concerns
  • Fuel-delivery concerns and related components
  • Belts, hoses and common wear items
  • Light-duty diesel work considered case by case
  • Cars, SUVs and light trucks
  • Other repairs reviewed individually
How we approach a problem

A trouble code is a clue. Not a diagnosis.

When diagnosis is needed, the goal is to narrow the failure down far enough that the repair makes sense before money gets spent on parts.

1
Start with the complaint

What the vehicle is doing, when it happens and what changed matters.

2
Test what can be tested

Codes, voltage, power, grounds, mechanical condition and other evidence guide the next step.

3
Explain what we found

You should understand why a repair is being recommended before you approve it.

Where the work happens

On-site when practical. Repair location when it makes sense.

We choose the setting based on the repair — not just convenience. Some jobs are perfectly reasonable at a home or workplace. Others need more room, equipment, teardown time or controlled conditions.

On-Site Work

Available when the location is safe and the repair can reasonably be completed there without compromising the job.

Repair Location

Larger, longer or more involved repairs can be scheduled at our repair location by appointment.

Not the Right Fit?

We will tell you. If another local mechanic or specialty shop is better equipped for the job, a good referral is better than forcing a repair that is outside the right lane.

A service list is not a promise to take every job

The right repair matters more than keeping every ticket.

Vehicle design, rust, prior repairs, required specialty equipment, parts availability and the exact scope of a job can all change whether we are the right fit. We would rather be upfront about that before work starts than learn it at your expense.

If we are not the best fit, we will say so. When possible, we are happy to point you toward another local mechanic or specialty shop better suited to the work.

Service questions

Before you schedule.

What if my repair is not listed on this page?

Ask anyway. This page covers common work, not every job we may be able to handle. Text the year, make, model and symptoms and we can tell you whether it is something we can look at.

Do you diagnose the problem before replacing parts?

When diagnosis is needed, yes — that is the goal. Some failures are obvious, but when the cause is uncertain we would rather test and narrow it down than replace unrelated parts and hope.

Can you work on the vehicle at my home or workplace?

Sometimes. On-site work is available when the location is safe and the repair can reasonably be completed there. Larger or more involved jobs are scheduled at our repair location.

Do you work on diesels?

Ask about light-duty diesel work. Availability depends on the specific repair, and work requiring specialized diesel equipment may be referred out when that is the better option.

What if you find more wrong after the repair starts?

We stop and contact you before additional work is added. You approve the changed repair plan and cost before we proceed beyond what you already authorized.

How are parts priced?

Parts are billed at the actual purchase price with no parts markup. When different quality levels make sense, available options can be discussed before ordering.

Not sure which service you need?

Send the symptoms. We’ll start there.

For the fastest answer, text the year, make, model, your location and what the vehicle is doing. Photos, videos and scan-tool codes can help too.
