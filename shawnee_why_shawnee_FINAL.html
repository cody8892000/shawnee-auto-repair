How we handle the work

The repair matters. So does everything around it.

Good service is more than being friendly when you drop off the keys. It means being clear about what we know, what we do not know yet, what you authorized, what changed, and what happens next.

1

Diagnose before guessing

We would rather spend time finding the cause than replace a stack of parts and hope one of them solves the problem. If more testing is needed, we tell you.

2

Approval before added work

If the repair changes, you hear about it first. We explain what was found and what it changes before additional work or cost becomes part of the job.

3

Spend where it makes sense

When different parts or repair paths make sense, we explain the tradeoffs. The goal is not automatically the cheapest option or the most expensive one—it is the choice that makes sense for the vehicle, the repair, and your budget.

4

Reputation over one ticket

We would rather earn the next ten years of your business than squeeze the maximum amount out of one repair.

If there is a concern

Tell us. We want the chance to look at it.

Vehicles are complicated, repairs do not always unfold exactly as expected, and misunderstandings can happen. What matters is what a business does next.

If something does not seem right after a repair—or you simply have a question about what was done—bring it up. We would rather hear about it, inspect what needs to be inspected, and explain what we find than have you leave wondering.

1Tell us what is going on. Call or text and explain what you are seeing, hearing, or feeling.
2We look into it. We review the repair and, when needed, inspect the vehicle so the conversation is based on what is actually happening.
3We explain what we find. If it relates to our work, is a separate problem, or needs more diagnosis, we tell you plainly.
4We work toward a fair next step. When something is ours to address, we want to address it. When it is not, we still want you to understand why.
The right fit matters

Not every job belongs here.

Some repairs are better handled by a shop with a particular specialty, piece of equipment, schedule, or setup. If that is the case, we would rather tell you than force a job that is not the right fit.

A good referral is still good service. The goal is to help move you toward the right solution, even when that means another reputable shop is better suited for the repair.
